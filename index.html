About the Project

Understand the problem, search for solutions

Globally, over 2 billion people cook with polluting fuels like firewood or charcoal, leading to household air pollution, 2-3 million untimely deaths per year, deforestation, and 1-3% of global green house gas emissions. Air pollution ranks as the second-highest risk factor for death and disability in Ghana, where the majorityof households burn solid fuels.

CHAP combines methods from public health, development economics, and energy research to study the health and climate impacts of clean cooking technologies and help evaluate and accelerate interventions in Ghana. Our work leverages national cooking fuel surveys, randomized controlled trials tracking exposure and health outcomes, field experiements studying how distance and price affect demand for clean cooking fuels, and the development of GasPay, a digital financial platform to help households save for and access LPG.

While this collaboration started in 2007, our most recent work is an RCT testing GasPay in Techiman, Bono East Region, since 2025.

Research

Areas of Investigation

Our research spans multiple interconnected themes from the health burden of indoor air pollution, to behavioral and economic barriers to clean fuel adoption, to building and testing digital tools to accelerate the energy transition.

01

Health Impacts of HAP

Air pollution is the second-highest risk factor for death and disability in Ghana. Our longitudinal RCT tracks impacts of LPG adoption on birth weight, infant growth, blood pressure, and lung function over 12 years of follow-up.

View paper →
02

Household Energy Survey

A nationwide survey of 7,398 primary cooks across all 16 regions of Ghana measuring cooking practices, stove stacking, fuel expenditures, and the socioeconomic determinants of LPG use and transition. Spending on charcoal is similar to spending on LPG.

View paper →
03

Rural LPG Demand

A randomized field study in Kintampo varying LPG prices and distances to exchange depots. Rural consumers' demand is highly sensitive to both. Convenience matters as much as cost. CRM creates opportunity for decentralized distribution.

View paper →
04

Urban Subsidies & Targeting

A two-stage experiment in Techiman testing targeted subsidies for small LPG cylinders used by lower-income households. Subsidy budgets can be reduced by 30% with no loss in demand. Low-income consumers may use LPG if they can overcome savings constraints.

View paper →
05

GasPay: Digital Finance for LPG

A digital payments platform linking consumers with LPG suppliers via dedicated savings wallets, mobile ordering, and savings bonuses. Preliminary RCT evidence from 1,800 participants shows GasPay increases any LPG use by 10–15% and reduces charcoal spending.

View paper →
06

Energy Transition & Climate

Modeling climate implications of clean cooking scenarios for Ghana. A full charcoal-to-LPG transition via GasPay could avoid 2.4 Mt CO₂e per year, or ~10% of Ghana's total estimated emissions. LPG is less expensive per MJ-delivered energy than charcoal or firewood.

View paper →
